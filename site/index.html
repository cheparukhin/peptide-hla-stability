Boltz-2 features improve peptide–HLA stability ranking

Can representations from a structure model improve peptide–HLA stability prediction? We fit lightweight supervised predictors to frozen Boltz-2 residue-pair features and compare them against sequence models for ranking measured complex stability within HLA alleles.

Biological context and modelling approach

Class I human leukocyte antigen (HLA) molecules present intracellular peptides on the cell surface for T-cell recognition. How long a peptide–HLA complex remains intact influences antigen presentation. Using existing assay data, we train small supervised predictors on internal residue-pair features extracted from frozen Boltz-2.

Cohort eligibility, nested selection, and representation controls

The development cohort comes from the original training split and spans 75 HLA allele/construct labels. Of these, 69 are eligible—having both at least 20 rows and varying labels—and receive equal weight in the primary endpoint. Nested cross-validation separates held-out peptide clusters from model fitting and selection.

For unseen peptide clusters on known alleles, the prespecified embedding selector—defined as inner-selected frozen-feature procedures with the original sequence ensemble as fallback—outperforms the competitive sequence selector across the 69 eligible alleles on the development panel. The conditional interval supports a within-allele ranking gain from Boltz-2 trunk representations.

Retaining position-aware token features improves ranking over pooling the same underlying tokens, cleanly supporting positional indexing. While the full 9×34 interface representation also outperforms contact-restricted pooled pairs, this comparison changes both groove coverage and positional resolution jointly rather than isolating a positional effect. Separately, pooling over a crystal-derived peptide–HLA contact mask offers no established advantage over shuffled residue-pair membership (+0.0007 [−0.0096, +0.0236]). The positive performance comparisons show that the learned representations carry predictive signal without establishing a specific physical contact mechanism behind the gain.

03 · Additional descriptors and learners

Tested descriptors and learners show no established extra gain

Adding ProteinMPNN inverse-folding scores, FoldX energy terms, pairwise ranking losses, or hybrid learners showed no established extra benefit beyond Boltz-2 features in tested comparisons. Confidence intervals span zero, which neither establishes equivalence nor rules out useful effects.

ProteinMPNN, FoldX, ranking-loss, and hybrid-learner comparisons

We next test whether supplementing frozen Boltz trunk embeddings with explicit inverse-folding likelihoods or physical energy terms adds predictive signal. On an identity-selected, allele-balanced 4,449-pair panel (at most 64 rows per allele, with 69 scoring alleles eligible under at least 20 rows and varying labels), matched two-member MLPs evaluate two augmentations: adding complex-context ProteinMPNN scores (C) to a reference of Boltz plus peptide-only ProteinMPNN scores (Boltz+P), and adding repaired FoldX energy descriptors to Boltz features alone. The full-cohort extension described next evaluates a different contrast (R5+P+C versus R5) rather than a sample-size change alone.

04 · Secondary sequence-model benchmark

Sequence stability scores distinguish eluted ligands from decoys

To test whether predicted stability transfers to antigen presentation, we evaluate the sequence stability ensemble on nine-residue MHC Motif Atlas ligands without fitting to elution labels. The benchmark comprises 81,600 positive peptide–HLA pairs and 816,000 human-proteome decoy pairs across 51 alleles (median per-allele AUPRC 0.7804), with all 5,633 unique peptide sequences in the stability dataset excluded from both classes.

Interpretation

Scope of evidence and next decisive experimental test

All stability evaluations rely on one existing scintillation proximity assay that tracks β2-microglobulin dissociation as a stability proxy and contains unresolved zero-valued labels. The models rank recorded stability rather than predicting calibrated half-lives or immunogenicity. The decisive next test is independent, blinded kinetic measurement using frozen models and raw replicate dissociation curves.

Conditional resampling intervals and three-comparison family adjustment

All confidence intervals resample saved out-of-fold predictions while holding fitted models and selection choices fixed, using peptide-cluster resampling for peptide holdout and crossed HLA-group by peptide-cluster resampling for transfer and adaptation. These conditional intervals omit variance from refitting models, collecting new measurements, and selecting follow-up studies adaptively. One three-comparison family spans 20-label R5 adaptation, ProteinMPNN complex context beyond peptide-only, and FoldX augmentation; the 98.33% intervals correct only that declared family, not project-wide exploration.

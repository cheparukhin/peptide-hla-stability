Boltz embeddings improve peptide–HLA stability ranking

Boltz-2 features improve within-allele ranking of peptide–HLA complex stability in development assessments and across held-out HLA groups. Adding twenty measurements per target allele further improves adaptation. Independent kinetic measurements are needed to test these findings.

Can representations from a structure model predict complex stability?

Class I human leukocyte antigen (HLA) molecules display intracellular peptides on the cell surface for T-cell recognition. How long a peptide–HLA complex remains intact influences antigen presentation. Ordering candidate peptides by measured stability within each allele therefore complements binding-affinity prediction.

The trunk is Boltz-2's main processing stage. Its embeddings are numerical features describing individual residues and residue pairs in context. We test whether these frozen features improve peptide stability ranking, measured by median within-allele Spearman correlation.

Nested cross-validation separates held-out peptide clusters from model fitting and selection.

For unseen peptide clusters on known alleles, the prespecified embedding selector—defined as inner-selected frozen-feature procedures with the original sequence ensemble as fallback—outperforms the competitive sequence selector across the 69 eligible alleles on the development panel. The conditional interval supports a within-allele ranking gain from Boltz-2 trunk representations.

Retaining position-aware token features improves ranking over pooling the same underlying tokens, cleanly supporting positional indexing. While the full 9×34 interface representation also outperforms contact-restricted pooled pairs, this comparison changes both groove coverage and positional resolution jointly rather than isolating a positional effect. Separately, pooling over a crystal-derived peptide–HLA contact mask offers no established advantage over shuffled residue-pair membership (+0.0007 [−0.0096, +0.0236]). The positive performance comparisons show that the learned representations carry predictive signal without establishing a specific physical contact mechanism behind the gain.

03 · Few-shot adaptation

Twenty measurements per target allele improve transfer

When a target HLA allele has limited experimental data, few-shot adaptation can adjust source predictions using a small support set of existing labels. Under strict joint holdout across 68 target alleles, adapting a Boltz-feature ridge model (R5) raises mean design-specific median within-allele Spearman correlation from 0.5296 without target labels to 0.5763 with 20 existing labels per allele, a paired improvement of +0.0468 (98.33% three-primary Bonferroni family interval [+0.0140, +0.1195]). In this separate retrospective ridge study, query identities are fixed across label budgets within each of three support designs (not identical across all three designs), and the intersection of eligible allele labels across designs gives 68 alleles. Because its source/query exclusions differ from the 69-allele MLP transfer benchmark, these scores are not a matched cross-study comparison.

04 · Additional descriptors and learners

Added complexity has no established benefit beyond Boltz

We next test whether supplementing frozen Boltz trunk embeddings with explicit inverse-folding likelihoods or physical energy terms adds predictive signal. On an identity-selected, allele-balanced 4,449-pair panel (at most 64 rows per allele, with 69 scoring alleles eligible under at least 20 rows and varying labels), matched two-member MLPs evaluate two augmentations: adding complex-context ProteinMPNN scores (C) to a reference of Boltz plus peptide-only ProteinMPNN scores (Boltz+P), and adding repaired FoldX energy descriptors to Boltz features alone. The full-cohort extension described next evaluates a different contrast (R5+P+C versus R5) rather than a sample-size change alone.

Interpretation

A useful development result; independent measurements must test generalisation

The evidence supports improved within-allele stability ranking inside this development corpus, but does not establish calibrated physical half-lives, immunogenicity prediction, or experimental screening savings. The underlying assay measures β2-microglobulin dissociation as a stability proxy, and zero-valued summary records may reflect censoring or measurement limitations that aggregated labels cannot resolve.

All confidence intervals resample saved out-of-fold predictions while holding fitted models and selection choices fixed, using peptide-cluster resampling for peptide holdout and crossed HLA-group by peptide-cluster resampling for transfer and adaptation. These conditional intervals omit variance from refitting models, collecting new measurements, and selecting follow-up studies adaptively. One three-comparison family spans 20-label R5 adaptation, ProteinMPNN complex context beyond peptide-only, and FoldX augmentation; the 98.33% intervals correct only that declared family, not project-wide exploration.

The decisive next experiment is an independent, blinded kinetic-assay panel evaluated with frozen models and a prespecified analysis plan. Retaining raw dissociation curves and experimental replicates is needed to distinguish rapid decay from censoring or assay failure, providing the independent confirmation that development-split evaluation cannot supply.

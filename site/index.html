Boltz embeddings improve peptide–HLA stability ranking

Structure-model representations improve ranking within a development dataset and under held-out HLA groups. Twenty measurements per target allele improve adaptation. Independent experimental confirmation is the next test.

Can representations from a structure model predict complex stability?

Peptide–HLA complexes display intracellular peptides to T cells. We study whether frozen Boltz-2 embeddings improve the ordering of peptides by experimentally measured complex stability. The endpoint is the median within-allele Spearman correlation, giving each eligible allele equal weight.

The development cohort contains 75 alleles; 69 have at least 20 observations for the primary endpoint. These pairs belong to the original training split. Nested assessment separates held-out peptide clusters from model fitting and selection.

03 · Few-shot adaptation

Twenty measurements per target allele improve transfer

Under strict joint holdout, adapting a Boltz-feature ridge model raises the score from 0.5296 without target labels to 0.5763 with 20 labels per allele. The paired improvement is +0.0468, with a three-primary Bonferroni interval of [+0.0140, +0.1195] at 98.33%.

04 · Additional descriptors and learners

Added complexity has no established benefit beyond Boltz

On a matched 4,449-pair subset, neither ProteinMPNN complex context nor repaired FoldX descriptors establishes an additional ranking benefit. For ProteinMPNN, the comparison adds complex-context scores to Boltz plus peptide-only ProteinMPNN scores. For FoldX, it adds repaired energy descriptors to Boltz alone.

Interpretation

A useful development result; independent measurements must test generalisation

The evidence supports improved ranking within this assay corpus. It does not establish calibrated half-lives, immunogenicity prediction, or experimental savings. The underlying assay uses β2-microglobulin dissociation as a proxy for peptide–HLA stability. Zero-valued records may reflect censoring or other measurement limitations; their cause cannot be resolved from the aggregated labels.

All intervals resample saved predictions while holding models and selection decisions fixed. Peptide-cluster resampling is used for peptide holdout; crossed HLA-group and peptide-cluster resampling is used for transfer and adaptation. The intervals omit repeated fitting, new assay measurements, and the project's adaptive choice of follow-up studies. The three-primary correction covers its declared family only.

The decisive next experiment is a blinded kinetic-assay panel evaluated with frozen models and a prespecified analysis. Raw curves and independent replicates should distinguish low stability, censoring, and assay failure. No independent cohort has yet confirmed these results.

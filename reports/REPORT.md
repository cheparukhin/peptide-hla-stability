# Internal Structural Foundation Model Representations Improve Peptide–HLA Class I Kinetic Stability Prediction and Cross-Allele Transfer

## Abstract

The kinetic stability of a peptide–HLA class I (pMHC-I) complex—quantified by its dissociation half-life ($t_{1/2} = \ln 2 / k_{\text{off}}$)—governs how long an antigen remains displayed at the cell surface for CD8$^+$ T-cell recognition and provides biophysical information distinct from equilibrium binding affinity ($K_d = k_{\text{off}} / k_{\text{on}}$; median within-allele Spearman $\rho = 0.580$ across $7,281$ dual-labeled complexes). Although sequence-based neural networks predict within-allele stability accurately for well-sampled HLA alleles, when and why biomolecular foundation models improve upon sequence predictors has remained unclear. Here we systematically evaluate sequence ensembles, protein language models (ESM-2 and PPI-adapted MINT), all-atom structure predictors (Boltz-2), inverse-folding likelihoods (ProteinMPNN), and empirical force-field energies (FoldX) across **28,166** experimentally measured 9-mer peptide–HLA complexes spanning **75** HLA alleles (Rasmussen et al., 2016) and **897,600** evaluated pairs in an external immunopeptidomics ligand elution benchmark across **51** alleles (MHC Motif Atlas).

On held-out peptide clusters ($5,565$ pairs, $67$ alleles), a 30-model multilayer perceptron (MLP) ensemble taking the 9-mer peptide and 34-residue HLA contact pseudosequence achieves a median within-allele Spearman $\rho$ of **0.7064**, outperforming a single network by $+0.0883$ ($\rho = 0.6181$) and matching an ensemble trained on the full 182-residue HLA $\alpha_1\alpha_2$ domain ($\rho = 0.6904$). Evaluated zero-shot on $81,600$ mass-spectrometry-eluted ligands against $816,000$ human proteome decoys across $51$ alleles, this stability predictor reaches a median **AUROC of 0.9656** (AUPRC $0.7804$), retains **0.9157 AUROC** against allele-swapped eluted-ligand decoys that control for antigen processing and presentability, and drops to **0.6965** when HLA pseudosequences are permuted. Paradoxically, standard foundation-model features fail to improve upon this sequence baseline: frozen **ESM-2** embeddings match the baseline ($\rho = 0.6979$, $\Delta\rho = -0.0085$), and summarizing $28,166$ predicted three-chain **Boltz-2** structures ($143.6$ A10G GPU-hours) via pocket contact counts, minimum distances, surface burial, and confidence metrics degrades ranking to **0.5947** ($\Delta\rho = -0.1117$). Controlled diagnostics reveal that while cross-chain self-attention in a PPI-adapted language model (MINT) detects a modest interface signal ($\Delta\rho = +0.0193$, $p = 5.0 \times 10^{-6}$), it is outweighed by degraded per-residue anchor specificity ($\Delta\rho = -0.0800$ vs ESM-2 650M); meanwhile, coarse 3D coordinate summaries fail even under L2-shrunk linear residual regression ($\Delta\rho \le +0.0084$) because canonical 9-mer backbones occupy nearly invariant heavy-atom coordinates inside the MHC cleft.

We resolve this paradox by showing that the failure of coarse structural features is a representation bottleneck rather than a limitation of the folding trunk. Extracting **position-resolved peptide–groove pair representations** directly from the frozen Boltz-2 trunk prior to coordinate diffusion preserves the residue-specific anchor conditioning that global pooling and scalar distance cutoffs erase. In nested 5-fold peptide-cluster cross-validation across the $19,716$-pair development cohort ($3,587$ peptide clusters, $69$ alleles), a pre-specified Boltz-2 trunk selector achieves $\rho = \mathbf{0.7212}$ versus $\rho = \mathbf{0.6668}$ for a competitive sequence selector spanning five sequence model families (**$\Delta\rho = +0.0543$**, $95\%$ CI $[+0.0314, +0.0808]$). Blending a linear trunk ridge probe with the sequence ensemble reaches **0.7238** ($\Delta\rho = +0.0569$), and retaining all $9 \times 34$ projected peptide–groove pair tokens reaches **0.7315**. Controlled ablations demonstrate that preserving individual peptide positions improves ranking by **$+0.3387$** ($[+0.3029, +0.3773]$) over dimension-matched pooled tokens, whereas a crystal-derived contact mask offers no advantage over randomly permuted groove subsets ($\Delta\rho = +0.0007$ $[-0.0096, +0.0236]$). Crucially, under leave-one-HLA-group-out cross-validation ($\ge 4$ contact-groove substitutions from all training alleles), sequence MLPs collapse ($\rho = 0.1605$) whereas matched Boltz-2 trunk MLPs reach **0.5667** (**$\Delta\rho = +0.4062$**, $95\%$ CI $[+0.3073, +0.4733]$); under simultaneous peptide-cluster and HLA-group holdout, ranking improves from $0.1242$ to **0.4980** (**$\Delta\rho = +0.3738$** $[+0.3092, +0.4638]$). Calibrating a frozen trunk ridge head with **20 measured peptides** per held-out allele further lifts joint-holdout ranking from **0.5296** to **0.5763** (**$\Delta\rho = +0.0468$**, $95\%$ CI $[+0.0227, +0.1035]$, $98.33\%$ Bonferroni CI $[+0.0140, +0.1195]$) through feature-weight rotation ($+0.0444$) rather than an additive allele offset ($+0.0024$). Augmenting trunk features with ProteinMPNN log-likelihoods, FoldX energies, or pairwise ranking losses yields no further gain.

## 1. Introduction

Cytotoxic CD8$^+$ T lymphocytes surveil the intracellular proteome by recognizing short peptides—predominantly 9-mers—bound within the polymorphic $\alpha_1\alpha_2$ cleft of human leukocyte antigen class I (HLA-I) heavy chains non-covalently complexed with $\beta_2$-microglobulin ($\beta_2\text{m}$). For a candidate viral or tumor neoantigen epitope to trigger a productive T-cell response, the assembled peptide–HLA class I (pMHC-I) heterotrimer must export from the endoplasmic reticulum and persist at the cell surface long enough to engage surveying T-cell receptors. Although computational immunology has historically emphasized equilibrium binding affinity ($K_d = k_{\text{off}} / k_{\text{on}}$) or qualitative mass-spectrometry (MS) ligand elution, the kinetic stability of the complex—quantified by its dissociation half-life $t_{1/2} = \ln 2 / k_{\text{off}}$—is a closer physical determinant of cell-surface residence time and immunogenicity (Harndahl et al., 2012; Rasmussen et al., 2016). Two ligands with identical equilibrium $K_d$ can differ substantially in their association ($k_{\text{on}}$) and dissociation ($k_{\text{off}}$) rate constants. Across $7,281$ construct-compatible peptide–HLA pairs measured for both quantitative binding affinity and dissociation half-life, experimental affinity ranks kinetic stability with a median within-allele Spearman correlation of only $\rho = 0.580$ (mean $\rho = 0.577$; [`reports/stage2c_affinity_ceiling.csv`](stage2c_affinity_ceiling.csv)).

Predicting pMHC-I kinetic stability exposes a fundamental tension between local sequence motifs and three-dimensional interface physics:

1. **Within-allele anchor dominance versus cross-allele groove polymorphism**: Within any single HLA allele, 9-mer complex stability is governed primarily by whether the side chains at primary anchor positions (typically P2 and P9, supplemented by auxiliary anchors at P1 and P3) satisfy the steric and electrostatic preferences of the B and F pockets. When hundreds of kinetic measurements are available for a target allele, compact sequence neural networks learn these allele-specific anchor preferences directly. When predicting stability for rare, under-sampled, or unseen HLA alleles, however, a predictor must infer how multiple simultaneous substitutions across the 34 polymorphic groove residues remodel pocket geometry and electrostatics—a non-additive cross-chain coupling where sequence interpolation breaks down.
2. **The fixed-backbone representation bottleneck in structural foundation models**: Modern protein language models (e.g., ESM-2) and all-atom biomolecular structure predictors (e.g., AlphaFold3, Boltz-2) encode rich evolutionary and geometric constraints. Yet 9-mer pMHC-I complexes present a deceptive regime for standard structural featurization: nearly all bound 9-mers adopt the same canonically anchored, extended backbone trace inside the MHC cleft, with conserved hydrogen-bond networks locking the peptide N- and C-termini at opposite ends of the groove. Summarizing predicted 3D structures via coarse heavy-atom contact counts, minimum pocket distances, global confidence scalars (pLDDT, ipTM, PAE), or chain-pooled embeddings therefore risks discarding residue-specific side-chain compatibility while preserving only small coordinate fluctuations.

Here we investigate this tension across $28,166$ kinetic stability measurements and $897,600$ immunopeptidomics evaluation pairs. Because published tools such as NetMHCstabpan (Rasmussen et al., 2016) were trained on the complete scintillation proximity assay dataset analyzed here, their released weights cannot serve as leak-free baselines on held-out partitions. We instead construct a rigorous hierarchy of leak-free sequence predictors (30-network one-hot/BLOSUM62 MLP ensembles, empirical-Bayes allele-centered ensembles, gradient-boosted decision trees, and within-allele pairwise rankers), demonstrate zero-shot biological transfer to natural MS ligand presentation under deconfounded decoy controls, diagnose why protein language models and coarse 3D descriptors fail, and show that extracting **position-resolved peptide–groove pair representations** directly from the frozen Boltz-2 trunk substantially improves both in-panel ranking and cross-allele transfer.

## 2. Dataset, Biophysical Signal Structure, and Evaluation Design

### 2.1 Kinetic Stability Dataset and Assay Architecture

All supervised stability experiments use the proximity-based dissociation dataset of Rasmussen et al. (*J. Immunol.*, 2016; curated in [`data/rasmussen_et_al_dataset.csv`](../data/rasmussen_et_al_dataset.csv) from `data/raw/dtu_stability_data.xlsx`). The curated cohort comprises **28,166** unique `(allele_raw, peptide)` records spanning **5,633** 9-mer peptides of canonical amino acids and **75** HLA class I allele strings: **72** wild-type alleles across 26 HLA-A, 34 HLA-B, 11 HLA-C, and 1 HLA-E\*01:01, plus **3** engineered disulfide-stabilized heavy-chain constructs (`HLA-A*02:01 C67S`, `HLA-A*24:02 C67S`, and `HLA-B*07:02 C67S`). Engineered constructs retain their exact mutated heavy-chain sequence (`domain_seq`) for structure prediction and full-domain models while mapping to their parent canonical 34-residue contact pseudosequence where appropriate (see [`docs/DATASETS.md`](../docs/DATASETS.md)).

The scintillation proximity assay (SPA) measures the dissociation of radiolabeled $^{125}\text{I}$-$\beta_2\text{m}$ from recombinant biotin-tagged pMHC-I complexes immobilized on streptavidin-coated donor beads at $37\,^\circ\text{C}$ after addition of excess unlabeled $\beta_2\text{m}$, reporting $t_{1/2}$ in hours as the geometric mean of two independent replicates. Because non-covalent $\beta_2\text{m}$ dissociation rapidly follows peptide unbinding from the $\alpha_1\alpha_2$ cleft, $^{125}\text{I}$-$\beta_2\text{m}$ decay provides a high-throughput kinetic readout of pMHC-I persistence. Continuous regression targets are modeled on the variance-stabilized natural-logarithm scale:
$$y = \ln(1 + t_{1/2}),$$
which maps $t_{1/2} = 0\text{ h}$ to $y = 0$ while preserving strict monotonic order for rank-based evaluation. Exactly **5,693** complexes (**20.21%**) are recorded at $t_{1/2} = 0.0\text{ h}$ (and only a single observation in the entire dataset lies strictly between $0.0\text{ h}$ and $0.1\text{ h}$, the lowest half-life reported in bulk at $443$ rows), corresponding to complexes that either dissociate faster than the assay's $0.1\text{ h}$ reporting floor or fail to refold productively prior to the chase phase.

### 2.2 Biophysical Structure of the Signal: Anchor Dominance vs. Groove Divergence

To characterize the physical structure of the stability landscape before fitting foundation models, we quantified the positional variance decomposition of $\log_{10} t_{1/2}$ across peptide positions P1–P9 ([`reports/position_eta2.csv`](position_eta2.csv)) and measured how sequence-model generalization decays with binding-groove divergence ([`reports/stage7_allele_holdout_strata_seq_pep_pseudo.csv`](stage7_allele_holdout_strata_seq_pep_pseudo.csv)):

1. **Anchor-dominated within-allele variance**: A one-way analysis of variance ($\eta^2$, the fraction of within-allele $\log_{10} t_{1/2}$ variance explained by the 20 amino-acid identities at a single peptide position) across all 75 alleles reveals extreme positional concentration. Primary anchor positions **P2** and **P9**—together with auxiliary anchors **P1** and **P3** in specific HLA supertypes, such as P3 in `HLA-A*01:01` ($\eta^2_{\text{P3}} = 0.251$) or P1 and P9 in `HLA-B*51:01` and `HLA-B*56:01` ($\eta^2_{\text{P9}} = 0.453\text{–}0.625$)—account for the vast majority of within-allele stability variance. Conversely, the solvent-exposed central bulge positions **P4–P8** exhibit high sequence entropy ($\approx 3.8\text{–}4.2\text{ bits}$, approaching the uniform 20-amino-acid ceiling of $4.32\text{ bits}$) and low marginal $\eta^2$ (typically $<0.03\text{–}0.06$). Consequently, any representation that pools across the 9 peptide tokens destroys the primary physical signal.
2. **Monotonic collapse of sequence models across divergent HLA grooves**: In leave-one-allele-out cross-validation across 68 eligible alleles ([`reports/stage7_allele_holdout_strata_seq_pep_pseudo.csv`](stage7_allele_holdout_strata_seq_pep_pseudo.csv)), stratifying held-out alleles by their minimum Hamming distance $d$ on the 34-residue contact pseudosequence to the closest training allele reveals a steep performance gradient:
   - **Near groove neighbors ($d \le 1$, $n = 25$ alleles, $8,547$ rows)**: The 30-MLP sequence ensemble achieves a median within-allele Spearman $\rho$ of **0.7415** ($95\%$ CI $[0.6417, 0.8026]$; or **0.7497** excluding the identical-pseudosequence pair `HLA-B*14:01`/`HLA-B*14:02`) and median top-10 precision of **1.00**.
   - **Intermediate groove neighbors ($d = 2\text{–}3$, $n = 23$ alleles, $7,279$ rows)**: Median Spearman $\rho$ drops to **0.5758** ($[0.4578, 0.7259]$) and top-10 precision to **0.80**.
   - **Distant groove neighbors ($d \ge 4$, $n = 20$ alleles, $6,595$ rows)**: Median Spearman $\rho$ collapses to **0.3390** ($[0.2978, 0.4908]$) and top-10 precision to **0.60**—a drop of **$\Delta\rho = -0.4025$** relative to $d \le 1$.

This distance decay shows that sequence networks rely on single-substitution interpolation from near-identical training grooves and motivates evaluating cross-allele generalization by holding out entire sequence groups of related alleles at Hamming distance $\le 3$.

### 2.3 Homology-Aware Partitioning and Two-Cohort Evaluation Protocol

To prevent homologous peptide leakage between training and evaluation partitions, all $5,633$ unique 9-mer peptides were grouped into connected components under single-linkage clustering at **Hamming distance $\le 3$** ($\ge 66.7\%$ sequence identity, yielding $4,562$ peptide clusters across the full dataset) and assigned to a fixed 70 / 10 / 20 partition ([`data/splits.csv`](../data/splits.csv)):
- **Development (training) cohort**: **19,716** pairs (**70.0%**) across **3,587** peptide clusters and **75** alleles (**69** eligible alleles having $\ge 20$ rows and $\ge 2$ distinct target values; the 6 alleles with $<20$ rows—`HLA-A*02:02`, `HLA-A*02:05`, `HLA-A*02:07`, `HLA-A*02:11`, `HLA-A*68:01`, and `HLA-B*58:01`—are retained in model fitting but excluded from per-allele median summaries). In this cohort, the $\ge 20$-row and $\ge 50$-row eligibility thresholds select the exact same 69 alleles.
- **Validation split**: **2,817** pairs (**10.0%**), with **68** eligible alleles ($2,756$ rows).
- **Test split**: **5,633** pairs (**20.0%**), with **67** eligible alleles ($5,565$ rows).

To preserve strict separation between initial hypothesis testing and subsequent representation design, evaluation is structured across two isolated regimes:
1. **Held-out test-split benchmark and external immunopeptidomics transfer**: Sequence baselines, frozen protein language models (ESM-2), coarse Boltz-2 3D geometry and confidence summaries (`R1`), left-censored Tobit calibration, and zero-shot MHC Motif Atlas ligand elution transfer were evaluated on the held-out test split (or the external elution corpus).
2. **Nested cross-validation and crossed HLA-group holdouts inside the 19,716-pair development cohort**: Because the initial validation and test splits were inspected when diagnosing why coarse 3D features (`R1`) failed, all subsequent Boltz-2 trunk representation engineering (`R2`–`R5`, `full_interface_pos`), model-family selectors, unseen-HLA transfer benchmarks, few-shot calibration studies, and ProteinMPNN/FoldX extensions were conducted strictly inside the **19,716-pair development cohort** without touching the validation or test splits:
   - **Nested 5-fold peptide-cluster cross-validation**: The $3,587$ development peptide clusters were partitioned into 5 outer folds balanced across alleles (`reports/further_research/round2/splits/`). All hyperparameter tuning, early stopping, and model-family selection were executed strictly inside inner cross-validation splits without access to outer-fold targets.
   - **Crossed $5 \times 5$ HLA-group $\times$ peptide-cluster holdouts**: All 75 alleles were partitioned into connected components under single-linkage clustering at **Hamming distance $\le 3$** on the 34-residue contact pseudosequence (`HLA_groups`, guaranteeing that every held-out allele lies at distance $d \ge 4$ from all training alleles) and assigned to 5 balanced HLA folds crossed with the 5 peptide-cluster folds ($25$ evaluation cells).

#### Primary Metric and Bootstrap Inference
Because peptide repertoires and half-life dynamic ranges differ substantially across alleles, pooling all complexes into a single global correlation conflates within-allele ligand ranking with between-allele mean offsets. Our primary endpoint is the **median within-allele Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$)** across eligible alleles ($\ge 20$ rows and $\ge 2$ unique $y$ values), weighting every allele equally. Secondary metrics include mean within-allele Spearman $\rho$, pooled root-mean-square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE), within-allele top-10% recall, and floor classification AUROC and Brier score. At the outset of the study, $\Delta\rho \ge +0.05$ was pre-specified as the benchmark for a practically substantial ranking improvement over the sequence baseline.

For every paired model contrast $\Delta\rho = \rho(\text{candidate}) - \rho(\text{baseline})$, uncertainty is quantified via non-parametric percentile bootstrap resampling (`2,000` draws):
- **Peptide-cluster holdouts** resample peptide clusters with replacement, recomputing per-allele Spearman correlations and paired median differences on each draw.
- **Unseen-HLA and few-shot adaptation holdouts** use a **crossed bootstrap** that simultaneously resamples HLA sequence groups and peptide clusters with replacement, capturing both allele-group and ligand-family sampling variability.
- **Family-wise multiplicity control**: For the three pre-declared primary follow-up hypotheses (20-shot target-allele calibration, ProteinMPNN complex-context increment, and FoldX energy increment), we report both pointwise $95\%$ confidence intervals and Bonferroni-corrected **$98.3333\%$** ($1 - 0.05/3$) family-wise intervals.

#### Three-Chain Ectodomain Folding and Trunk Extraction in Boltz-2
In an initial structural pilot, folding the isolated 182-residue HLA $\alpha_1\alpha_2$ domain without the supporting $\alpha_3$ immunoglobulin domain and $\beta_2\text{m}$ subunit destabilized the $\beta$-sheet floor. We therefore folded all $28,166$ complexes in **Boltz-2** (`boltz==2.2.1`, 1 recycling iteration, 25 diffusion steps, `143.6` A10G GPU-hours across 25 parallel Modal containers; [`reports/gpu_decision.csv`](gpu_decision.csv)) as complete three-chain heterotrimers comprising Chain A (full HLA heavy-chain ectodomain), Chain B (99-residue $\beta_2\text{m}$), and Chain C (9-mer peptide), achieving a $100\%$ completion rate ($28,166 / 28,166$ complexes) with precomputed heavy-chain and $\beta_2\text{m}$ MSAs and single-sequence peptide conditioning.

Prior to stochastic coordinate diffusion, the deterministic Boltz-2 trunk outputs a per-token single representation $s \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times 384}$ and a directed pairwise representation $z \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times L \times 128}$. Let $\mathcal{P} = \{1,\dots,9\}$ index the 9 peptide tokens, $\mathcal{D}_{182} = \{1,\dots,182\}$ index the mature HLA $\alpha_1\alpha_2$ groove tokens, and $\mathcal{G}_{34} \subset \mathcal{D}_{182}$ denote the 34 canonical NetMHCpan polymorphic contact positions. For each peptide position $p \in \mathcal{P}$ and groove residue $g \in \mathcal{D}_{182}$, we form the bidirectional 256-channel pair vector $u_{p,g} = [z_{p,g};\, z_{g,p}] \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 128}$:
- **`R4` ($2,304\text{-d}$)** averages $u_{p,g}$ uniformly across all 182 groove residues for each peptide position $p \in \{1,\dots,9\}$:
  $$\mathbf{r}_4 = \operatorname{vec}\!\Biggl(\biggl[\frac{1}{182}\sum_{g \in \mathcal{D}_{182}} u_{p,g}\biggr]_{p=1}^{9}\Biggr) \in \mathbb{R}^{9 \times 2 \times 128}.$$
- **`R5` ($1,024\text{-d}$)** restricts $g$ to the 34 contact positions $\mathcal{G}_{34}$ and defines for each peptide position $p$ a reference contact subset $M_p \subset \mathcal{G}_{34}$ as the union of groove residues within heavy-atom distance $\le 4.5\,\text{\AA}$ of position $p$ across five reference pMHC-I crystal structures (`1X7Q`, `1XR9`, `4QRU`, `5N6B`, `7LFZ`, yielding $5\text{–}12$ contacting groove residues per peptide position; [`docs/STRUCTURES.md`](../docs/STRUCTURES.md)). Defining the pocket-averaged pair state $\bar{u}_p = \frac{1}{|M_p|}\sum_{g \in M_p} u_{p,g} \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times 128}$, `R5` preserves primary and auxiliary anchors P1, P2, and P9 at single-residue resolution while averaging the high-entropy central core P3–P8 with equal weight:
  $$\mathbf{r}_5 = \operatorname{vec}\!\Biggl(\biggl[\bar{u}_1,\; \bar{u}_2,\; \bar{u}_9,\; \frac{1}{6}\sum_{p=3}^{8}\bar{u}_p\biggr]\Biggr) \in \mathbb{R}^{4 \times 2 \times 128}.$$

All representations in the `R0`–`R5` and `full_interface` comparison ladder are evaluated with an identical downstream learner recipe: a 30-member ensemble ($5\text{ peptide-cluster early-stopping folds} \times 6\text{ seeds}$) of standardized 64-unit ReLU MLPs ($\text{L2} \in \{1, 10^{-2}, 10^{-4}\}$ selected on inner folds, Adam $\text{lr} = 10^{-3}$, batch size $256$, patience $15$), alongside deterministic L2-regularized linear ridge probes ($\alpha \in \{10^4, 10^2, 1\}$).

#### Direct Overlap and Zero-Shot Transfer Across 897,600 Immunopeptidomics Pairs
To verify that our in vitro kinetic stability models capture physiological antigen presentation, we intersected the Rasmussen et al. dataset with the MHC Motif Atlas of liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) eluted ligands. Across the **54** shared HLA alleles ($151,170$ eluted 9-mers and $21,663$ stability-measured 9-mers), the **140** directly overlapping `(allele, peptide)` complexes across $35$ alleles (`data/processed/elution_overlap_pairs.parquet`) exhibit a **7.2-fold longer median dissociation half-life** than non-eluted measured peptides (**$7.90\text{ h}$** vs **$1.10\text{ h}$**, mean $11.55\text{ h}$ vs $2.62\text{ h}$; two-sided Mann–Whitney $U = 2.38 \times 10^6$, $p = 6.0 \times 10^{-31}$, common-language effect size $= 0.784$) and a **10-fold lower zero-stability floor rate** (**$2.1\%$** [$3/140$] vs **$21.2\%$** [$4,562/21,523$], Fisher's exact $p = 1.6 \times 10^{-10}$). Within the $21$ individual alleles containing both eluted and non-eluted measured ligands, the median within-allele half-life difference is **$+4.25\text{ h}$** (Wilcoxon signed-rank $p = 4.5 \times 10^{-6}$), demonstrating that the longevity advantage is a true within-allele kinetic property rather than an artifact of allele composition.

Because MS ligand elution reflects upstream source-protein abundance, proteasomal cleavage, and TAP transport in addition to pMHC-I stability, incorporating eluted ligands as training pseudo-labels would confound $k_{\text{off}}$ with antigen-processing biases. Three conclusions follow from this external evaluation:
1. **Zero-shot discrimination of naturally presented ligands**: Without exposure to any elution data, the 30-MLP kinetic stability ensemble separates naturally eluted ligands from human proteome decoys at a median per-allele **AUROC of 0.9656** (minimum $0.9037$ across all $51$ alleles) and a median **AUPRC of 0.7804** ($8.6\times$ above the $0.0909$ base rate), achieving **93.9% precision** in the top 1% of predicted half-lives ($10.33\times$ enrichment over random). Ensembling improves median AUPRC by $+0.1128$ over a single stability MLP ($0.7804$ vs $0.6676$).
2. **Deconfounding groove recognition from generic antigen processing**: Because random proteome 9-mers include peptides from low-abundance proteins or sequences refractory to proteasomal cleavage and TAP transport, we constructed an **allele-swapped decoy control** in which the $10\times$ negative decoys for each target allele are drawn exclusively from true MS-eluted ligands of *other* HLA alleles in the panel—peptides already proven to be expressed, cleaved, transported, and MS-detectable in human cells. 3. **Biophysical groove-similarity gradient and wrong-allele collapse**: Stratifying the 51 alleles in the allele-swapped control by the median contact-pseudosequence Hamming distance $d_H$ between the target allele and the donor alleles of its swapped decoys (split at the panel median $d_H = 14$ across the 34 groove positions) reveals a clear biophysical gradient: target alleles contrasted against distant donor grooves ($d_H > 14$, $n=25$) reach a median **AUROC of 0.9562** (AUPRC $0.7330$), whereas alleles contrasted against biophysically similar donor grooves ($d_H \le 14$, $n=26$) drop to **0.8906** (AUPRC $0.5067$) because closely related HLA supertypes share overlapping anchor preferences. Cyclically permuting the HLA pseudosequences across the 51 alleles collapses median AUROC to **0.6965** (AUPRC $0.1726$).

### 3.2 Why Naive Foundation-Model Features Fail: Protein Language Models and Coarse 3D Summaries

Against this validated sequence baseline, standard foundation-model featurization strategies on the held-out test split failed to improve ranking (Table 2, bottom):

1. **Single-chain and PPI-adapted protein language models (`ESM-2` and `MINT`)**: Replacing or concatenating the sequence inputs with frozen **ESM-2 35M** per-residue embeddings at the 9 peptide and 34 contact positions yields a test median $\rho$ of **0.6979** ($\Delta\rho = -0.0085$ $[-0.0291, +0.0233]$) and **0.6816** ($\Delta\rho = -0.0248$). Scaling to **ESM-2 150M** (validation $\rho = 0.6713$; [`reports/stage3e_comparisons.csv`](stage3e_comparisons.csv)) or passing a concatenated single-chain `peptide + linker + HLA` construct into ESM-2 (validation $\rho = 0.6820$; [`reports/stage3f_comparisons.csv`](stage3f_comparisons.csv)) likewise failed to surpass the sequence ensemble.
   To test whether a language model explicitly pre-trained on protein–protein interactions (PPIs) with cross-chain self-attention can capture peptide–groove coupling, we compared **MINT** (an ESM-2 650M architecture adapted on $96\text{M}$ STRING PPI pairs) against base **ESM-2 650M** across the 5-fold nested training cohort ($19,647$ construct-valid rows, $69$ alleles; [`reports/further_research/phase_c_frozen_summary.csv`](further_research/phase_c_frozen_summary.csv) and [`phase_c_frozen_deltas.csv`](further_research/phase_c_frozen_deltas.csv)):
   - **Cross-chain self-attention detects a genuine but modest interface signal**: Holding the MINT 650M weights and downstream head architecture fixed, enabling joint cross-chain attention between the 9-mer peptide and HLA chains ($\rho = 0.4044$) outperformed encoding the two chains separately ($\rho = 0.3851$) by **$\Delta\rho = +0.0193$** ($95\%$ CI $[+0.0038, +0.0830]$, improving $52/69$ alleles, Wilcoxon $p = 5.0 \times 10^{-6}$).
   - **Generic PPI pre-training degrades per-residue anchor specificity**: Despite this positive cross-chain increment, independent-chain MINT ($\rho = 0.3851$) performed substantially worse than base ESM-2 650M ($\rho = 0.4651$, **$\Delta\rho = -0.0800$** $[-0.1017, -0.0259]$) and ESM-2 35M ($\rho = 0.4673$), leaving all frozen language-model arms well below the sequence ensemble ($\rho = 0.6493$ on the identical folds). Thus, evolutionary single-chain or globular-PPI pre-training does not resolve fine-grained peptide–groove anchor energetics.

2. **Coarse 3D structural and confidence summaries (`R1`)**: Combining sequence features with $50$ biophysically motivated descriptors extracted from the diffused Boltz-2 3D coordinates and confidence heads (`R1`: per-position heavy-atom contact counts $<4.0\,\text{\AA}$, minimum pocket distances, buried surface area proxy, pLDDT, pTM, ipTM, and interface PAE) reduced test median Spearman $\rho$ from $0.7064$ to **0.5947** ($\Delta\rho = -0.1117$ $[-0.1339, -0.0575]$; [`reports/stage5_structural_bootstrap.csv`](stage5_structural_bootstrap.csv)).
   We conducted two controlled follow-up experiments ([`reports/further_research/phase_d_pose_reliability.csv`](further_research/phase_d_pose_reliability.csv) and [`phase_d_residual_selection_phase_a.csv`](further_research/phase_d_residual_selection_phase_a.csv)) to determine whether this degradation resulted from (a) stochastic diffusion coordinate noise or (b) neural network co-adaptation when concatenating geometry features with raw sequence:
   - **Multi-seed variance partitioning rules out coordinate jitter**: Re-running Boltz-2 across multiple stochastic diffusion seeds on a stratified complex panel and partitioning variance within each allele showed that a single diffusion seed already captures a median **$99.1\%$** of the within-allele variance across all $109$ geometric and confidence descriptors (intraclass reliability $0.8996\text{–}0.9999$; $99.6\%$ for anchor contact counts and $98.1\%$ for interface PAE and ipTM).
   - **L2-shrunk linear residual regression rules out neural co-adaptation**: To eliminate neural co-adaptation completely, we froze the out-of-fold sequence ensemble predictions ($\rho = 0.6596$) and fitted nested L2-penalized ridge regressions on up to $109$ Boltz-2 geometry and directional peptide$\leftrightarrow$groove PAE/confidence features strictly to predict the **residual error** $r = y - \hat{y}_{\text{seq}}$. Even under optimal L2 shrinkage, the best confidence-residual model (using 19 directional PAE, ipTM, and pLDDT terms) shifted 5-fold CV median $\rho$ by only **$+0.0084$** ($95\%$ CI $[-0.0055, +0.0124]$), while the anchor-geometry residual model selected infinite regularization ($\alpha = \infty$, zero structural weight) in $5/5$ folds ($\Delta\rho = 0.0000$).

Together, these diagnostics establish that coarse 3D summaries fail for a fundamental biophysical reason: because all bound 9-mer peptides adopt a nearly invariant canonical backbone trace inside the MHC-I groove, scalar heavy-atom distances and global confidence metrics vary minimally across ligands and discard the chemical identity and electrostatic compatibility of the side chains occupying the pockets.

### 3.4 Mechanistic Ablations: Positional Anchor Indexing vs. Crystal Contact Masking

Why do `R4`, `R5`, and `full_interface_pos` succeed where `R1` and `R2` failed? Three pre-specified architectural ablations inside the nested 5-fold cross-validation framework isolate the physical and representational mechanisms ([`reports/further_research/round2/results/main/paired_comparisons.csv`](further_research/round2/results/main/paired_comparisons.csv)):

1. **Preserving peptide positional indexing vs. chain pooling (`+0.3387`)**:
   - Chain-pooled trunk single representations (`R2_mlp`, $\rho = 0.4219$; `R2_ridge`, $\rho = 0.3694$) collapse far below sequence baselines because averaging across the 9 peptide tokens erases whether a hydrophobic or charged side chain occupies primary anchor positions P2/P9 or solvent-facing bulge positions P4–P8.
   - To rule out dimensionality or projection artifacts, we compared `R3_mlp` ($1,528\text{-d}$, which projects each of the 9 peptide and 182 groove single-state tokens from $384 \to 8$ channels) against `R3_pooled_matched_mlp` ($1,528\text{-d}$, which tiles the peptide-chain mean across the 9 peptide slots and the groove mean across the 182 groove slots before applying the identical orthonormal projection matrix and 30-MLP learner). Preserving positional indexing improves median within-allele Spearman $\rho$ from **0.3165** to **0.6552**—a paired gain of **$\Delta\rho = +0.3387$** ($95\%$ CI $[+0.3029, +0.3773]$).
   - Transitioning from projected single-chain tokens (`R3_mlp`, $0.6552$) to unprojected 128-channel directed peptide–groove pair tokens (`R4_mlp`, $0.7136$) adds a further **$\Delta\rho = +0.0584$** ($[+0.0419, +0.0882]$).

2. **Groove-position resolution vs. channel capacity (`+0.1663`)**:
   - Across the $9 \times 34$ peptide–contact-groove interface, projecting each directed pair vector from $128 \to 4$ channels while keeping all $9 \times 34$ pairs distinct (`full_interface_pos_mlp`, $2,448\text{-d}$) achieves **0.7315**.
   - When those 4-channel projected vectors are averaged across the crystal contact mask for each peptide position and tiled across the 34 groove columns to match dimensionality (`full_interface_pooled_mlp`, $2,448\text{-d}$, which retains peptide positions P1–P9 but collapses groove-position specificity into $9 \times 2 \times 4 = 72$ effective channels), performance drops to **0.5653** (**$\Delta\rho = +0.1663$**, $95\%$ CI $[+0.1378, +0.1992]$).
   - Crucially, when all **128 unprojected pair channels** are retained per direction before groove averaging (`R4_mlp` $0.7136$, `R5_mlp` $0.7210$), pooling across groove residues loses only $0.0105\text{–}0.0179$ relative to the full $9 \times 34$ interface ($0.7315$), because the 128-channel pair fiber has sufficient capacity to preserve pocket-specific conditioning through a linear mean.

3. **Crystal contact mask vs. shuffled HLA groove subsets (`+0.0007`)**:
   - `R5` ($1,024\text{-d}$) compresses `R4` ($2,304\text{-d}$) by keeping anchors P1, P2, and P9 separate while averaging the central core P3–P8, and by averaging each peptide position over its crystal-contacting HLA groove subset $M_p \subset \mathcal{G}_{34}$.
   - To test whether the crystal-derived spatial contact mask $M$ drives `R5`'s edge over `R4` ($0.7210$ vs $0.7136$), we evaluated `R5_shuffled_contact_mlp`, which preserves the exact $[\text{P1}, \text{P2}, \text{P9}, \operatorname{mean}(\text{P3–P8})]$ peptide structure and the exact subset cardinalities $|M_p|$ but permutes the 34 HLA groove columns randomly.
   - `R5_shuffled_contact_mlp` achieves **0.7203**, yielding a null difference of **$\Delta\rho = +0.0007$** ($95\%$ CI $[-0.0096, +0.0236]$). Thus, `R5` succeeds because it **allocates dedicated representation capacity to anchor positions P1, P2, and P9** while compressing the low-$\eta^2$ central bulge P3–P8—not because of the hand-crafted crystal contact mask. Within the Boltz-2 trunk, triangle multiplication and axial attention have already propagated pocket context across the groove tokens.

When entire HLA sequence groups ($d \ge 4$ groove mutations from any training allele) are held out, every sequence-only model family—including the 30-MLP sequence ensemble ($0.1709$ on HLA-group holdout; $0.1350$ on joint holdout), the architecture-matched sequence MLP `R0_mlp` ($0.1605$ and $0.1242$), and the full competitive sequence selector ($0.1921$ and $0.1085$)—collapses toward near-random ranking. Two mechanistic conclusions emerge from this experiment:
1. **Twenty target-allele measurements significantly improve cross-allele ranking**: Adding $20$ support peptides raises the `R5` ridge median within-allele Spearman $\rho$ from **0.5296** to **0.5763**, a paired gain of **$\Delta\rho = +0.0468$** whose pointwise $95\%$ crossed CI ($[+0.0227, +0.1035]$) and 3-primary Bonferroni-adjusted $98.3333\%$ CI ($[+0.0140, +0.1195]$) both exclude zero. 2. **Calibration operates by rotating interface feature weights rather than shifting an allele intercept**: Because Spearman rank correlation is computed *within* each allele, shifting all predictions for a target allele by an additive scalar constant cannot alter within-allele peptide ordering (apart from minor shrinkage boundary effects). Indeed, a scalar-offset-only adaptation control changes ranking by just **$+0.0024$** ($[-0.0062, +0.0239]$), and full feature-weight ridge adaptation outperforms the offset control by **$\Delta\rho = +0.0444$** ($95\%$ CI $[+0.0165, +0.0928]$). The $20$ support measurements re-orient the $1,024$ `R5` pair channels to match the target allele's specific anchor-pocket priorities.

## 4. Secondary and Negative Investigations

To provide a transparent scientific record, we detail four secondary investigations in which plausible 3D scoring functions, ranking objectives, censored likelihoods, or auxiliary datasets either yielded null ranking differences or improved probability calibration without altering ligand rank order.

### 4.2 Pairwise Ranking Objectives and Hybrid Model Families

Because our primary metric is within-allele Spearman rank correlation, we tested whether training predictors directly with a pairwise ranking loss or combining neural and tree-based learners improves over standard mean-squared-error (MSE) regression:
- **Contrast between raw sequence space and `R5` trunk space**: On raw one-hot sequence features, a CatBoost model trained with a within-allele `PairLogit` ranking loss ($\rho = 0.6305$) outperformed a single-encoding sequence MLP ($\rho = 0.6010$) by **$\Delta\rho = +0.0295$** ($95\%$ CI $[+0.0150, +0.0596]$; [`reports/further_research/phase_a_a4_comparisons.csv`](further_research/phase_a_a4_comparisons.csv)), motivating the inclusion of boosted trees and pairwise rankers inside our `competitive_sequence_selector` (where the full 30-MLP one-hot+BLOSUM62 ensemble reaches $0.6615$).
- **Within-allele RankNet objective on `R5`**: On the full $19,716$-pair cohort, replacing MSE with a within-allele logistic pairwise ranking loss on `R5` shifted the single-family MLP score from **0.6955** to **0.7026** (**$\Delta\rho = +0.0071$**, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0051, +0.0098]$; [`reports/finalization/ranking/comparisons.csv`](finalization/ranking/comparisons.csv)).
- **Hybrid tree/neural selector on `R5`**: Allowing inner cross-validation to select among MSE MLPs, RankNet MLPs, CatBoost trees, ridge regression, and their linear blends on `R5` yielded **0.6999** versus **0.6955** for the standard MSE MLP (**$\Delta\rho = +0.0044$**, $95\%$ CI $[-0.0055, +0.0100]$; [`reports/finalization/hybrids_cloud_v1/comparisons.csv`](finalization/hybrids_cloud_v1/comparisons.csv)). Because `R5` already linearizes the stability landscape effectively ($\rho = 0.6776$ with linear ridge alone), specialized ranking objectives or tree hybrids provide negligible additional gain.

### 4.3 Left-Censored Tobit Calibration and Auxiliary Affinity Supervision

#### Left-Censored Tobit Regression for the 20.2% Assay Floor
In the Rasmussen et al. dataset, $20.21\%$ of complexes ($5,693 / 28,166$) are recorded at $t_{1/2} = 0.0\text{ h}$ below the assay's $t_{\text{floor}} = 0.1\text{ h}$ reporting limit ($c = \ln(1 + 0.1) \approx 0.0953$ on the $y = \ln(1 + t_{1/2})$ scale; sensitivity-swept across $t_{\text{floor}} \in \{0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.30\}\text{ h}$). Standard MSE regression treats $y = 0$ as an exact point measurement, pulling predictions toward zero with full quadratic weight even when the latent half-life lies below the detection threshold. Under a left-censored Gaussian (Tobit) observation model with network output $\hat{\mu}_i$ and learned homoscedastic scale $\sigma$, the per-observation negative log-likelihood is:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tobit}}(\hat{\mu}_i, \sigma; y_i) = -\mathbb{I}(t_{1/2,i} \ge t_{\text{floor}})\,\ln\!\Biggl[\frac{1}{\sigma}\phi\!\left(\frac{y_i - \hat{\mu}_i}{\sigma}\right)\Biggr] - \mathbb{I}(t_{1/2,i} < t_{\text{floor}})\,\ln\Phi\!\left(\frac{c - \hat{\mu}_i}{\sigma}\right),$$
where $\phi$ and $\Phi$ denote the standard normal density and cumulative distribution functions, and the ensemble floor probability is evaluated as the $K=30$-member Gaussian mixture $\hat{P}(\text{floor}_i) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Phi\!\bigl((c - \hat{\mu}_{i,k})/\hat{\sigma}_k\bigr)$.

Evaluating this Tobit objective on the test split ([`reports/stage7_censored_calibration.csv`](stage7_censored_calibration.csv)) and in nested 5-fold cross-validation ([`reports/further_research/phase_b_paired.csv`](further_research/phase_b_paired.csv)) clarifies the relationship between censoring calibration and rank correlation:
- **The ranking–calibration trade-off under NLL early stopping**: When early stopping is governed by validation Tobit NLL, the test-split Brier score for floor classification drops by more than half—from **0.2055** (standard MSE ensemble) to **0.0970** ($\Delta\text{Brier} = -0.1085$ $[-0.1169, -0.0999]$; expected calibration error drops from $0.273$ to $0.053$)—but within-allele Spearman ranking drops by **$\Delta\rho = -0.0365$** ($[-0.0628, -0.0150]$ on test; $-0.0515$ in nested CV) because later training epochs adjust $\hat{\mu}$ and $\sigma$ to sharpen the censoring boundary at the expense of above-floor ordering.
- **Resolution via decoupled above-floor MSE early stopping**: Training with the Tobit likelihood while early-stopping exclusively on **above-floor validation MSE** preserves within-allele ranking ($\rho = 0.6507$ vs $0.6615$ in nested CV, $\Delta\rho = -0.0108$ $[-0.0242, +0.0036]$) while significantly improving floor-probability Brier skill over a fixed Gaussian $\sigma$ derived from inner out-of-fold uncensored residuals (**$+0.0165$** $[+0.0138, +0.0205]$), Platt scaling (**$+0.0100$** $[+0.0080, +0.0124]$), and isotonic regression (**$+0.0077$** $[+0.0061, +0.0097]$).

#### Auxiliary Equilibrium Affinity Supervision
We also tested whether augmenting stability training with external equilibrium binding affinity measurements ($140,855$ curated class I IC$_{50}$/$K_d$ records from MHCflurry / BD2013 in [`data/data_augmentation_iedb/affinity_reference_75alleles.csv`](../data/data_augmentation_iedb/affinity_reference_75alleles.csv) and [`data/augmentation/measured_affinity.csv`](../data/augmentation/measured_affinity.csv)) improves stability ranking. Padding under-represented alleles with censored weak-binder negatives ([`reports/stage2b_deltas.csv`](stage2b_deltas.csv)) or joint multitask learning with shared trunk layers and separate stability and affinity heads ([`reports/stage2c_deltas_ensemble_pep_pseudo.csv`](stage2c_deltas_ensemble_pep_pseudo.csv), [`reports/stage3b_deltas.csv`](stage3b_deltas.csv)) shifted validation median Spearman $\rho$ by at most **$+0.0055$** ($[-0.0185, +0.0226]$) for the sequence ensemble and **$+0.0044$** for ESM-2. Because equilibrium affinity ($K_d$) and kinetic stability ($t_{1/2}$) share only moderate within-allele rank correlation ($\rho = 0.580$), supervising on $K_d$ does not sharpen $k_{\text{off}}$ ordering for alleles that already have direct kinetic measurements.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

### Why Internal Trunk Representations Succeed Where Coarse Structural Summaries Fail
A central conclusion of this study is that *how* a biomolecular foundation model is interrogated matters as much as the model itself. In peptide–MHC class I complexes, the 9-mer ligand adopts a canonically anchored, extended conformation across nearly all alleles: conserved hydrogen-bond networks tether the peptide N- and C-termini at opposite ends of the groove, while primary anchor side chains (typically P2 and P9) project into polymorphic pockets along the $\alpha_1\alpha_2$ cleft. Because the peptide backbone occupies nearly invariant heavy-atom coordinates across complexes, summarizing a predicted 3D structure via coarse distance cutoffs or global confidence scalars (`R1`) fails even under heavily regularized linear residual regression ($\Delta\rho \le +0.0084$), and chain-level mean pooling (`R2`) erases which amino acid occupies the B and F pockets. Slicing the deterministic Boltz-2 trunk pair tensor $z$ by peptide position (`R4`, `R5`, `full_interface_pos`) preserves the learned 128-channel chemical and geometric compatibility of each anchor pocket.

Equally informative is our negative contact-mask ablation (`R5` vs `R5_shuffled_contact`, $\Delta\rho = +0.0007$). Because the Boltz-2 trunk applies iterative triangle multiplication and axial attention across all residue pairs before outputting $z$, each groove token's pair state already integrates structural context from neighboring pocket residues. Consequently, keeping peptide anchor positions P1, P2, and P9 separate from the central bulge P3–P8 is essential ($+0.166$ to $+0.339$ over pooled tokens), whereas restricting each peptide position to a hand-curated subset of crystal-contacting HLA residues is unnecessary once all 128 pair channels are retained.

### Scope Boundaries and Limitations
Several experimental and statistical scope boundaries frame the interpretation of our findings:
1. **Single assay platform and floor censoring**: All supervised training and evaluation labels derive from a single high-throughput platform—the Rasmussen et al. (2016) scintillation proximity assay—which measures $^{125}\text{I}$-$\beta_2\text{m}$ dissociation at $37\,^\circ\text{C}$ as a surrogate for peptide off-rate and provides only two-replicate geometric means without raw dissociation curves. Moreover, $20.21\%$ of records lie at $t_{1/2} = 0\text{ h}$; from the published summary table alone, rapid post-assembly dissociation cannot be distinguished from failed complex refolding prior to the assay.
2. **Foundation-model pre-training exposure**: When HLA sequence groups are held out from supervised stability fitting (Sections 3.5 and 3.6), they are strictly unseen by the downstream stability predictor, but pMHC crystal structures and HLA heavy-chain sequences were present in the Protein Data Bank and sequence databases used to pre-train Boltz-2. Our transfer benchmarks therefore measure how effectively frozen foundation-model representations bridge unseen HLA groups during supervised kinetic stability learning, rather than de novo folding of an unseen protein fold.
3. **Conditional bootstrap intervals and two-cohort study design**: Bootstrap confidence intervals resample peptide clusters (or crossed HLA groups and peptide clusters) on the fixed cohort conditional on the fitted models and selection rules. In addition, because the trunk representation hierarchy (`R2`–`R5`, `full_interface_pos`) was designed after observing that coarse Boltz-2 geometry summaries (`R1`) degraded ranking on the initial split, all trunk comparisons were restricted to nested cross-validation inside the $19,716$-pair development cohort.
4. **Prospective wet-lab confirmation**: While our zero-shot evaluation on $897,600$ MHC Motif Atlas complexes establishes strong biological transfer to natural ligand presentation ($0.9656$ AUROC against proteome decoys; $0.9157$ against allele-swapped eluted decoys), definitive validation of the Boltz-2 trunk advantage ($\Delta\rho = +0.054$ in-panel; $+0.35\text{–}0.41$ across unseen HLA groups; $+0.047$ with 20-shot calibration) requires a prospective, blinded wet-lab dissociation study with replicate-level decay curves across held-out HLA alleles and de novo peptide clusters (see [`reports/finalization/INDEPENDENT_ASSAY_BRIEF.md`](finalization/INDEPENDENT_ASSAY_BRIEF.md) for the pre-specified experimental protocol).

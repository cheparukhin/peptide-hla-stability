# Proposed bounded Modal finalization amendment

Design only, 12 October 2026. This document neither launches work nor supplies an execution authorization. It replaces the placement of future heavy work, not the frozen scientific graph. Root must bind a tested implementation, exact inputs, provider resources and absolute deadlines in separate phase authorizations before execution.

## Minimum implementation

Use one CPU-only Modal Sandbox at a time, with four separately authorized phases: transfer report; hybrid engineering pilot; hybrid scientific fitting; hybrid report. Each Sandbox runs a small supervisor and ordinary subprocesses. The main phase owns at most two fit subprocesses, each restricted to two numerical threads; the pilot owns one. Do not build a distributed fitting service, general queue or automatic recovery platform for ten jobs.

CPU Sandboxes are documented as non-preemptible; Functions may restart on both preemption and container crashes even if ordinary retries are disabled. The higher Sandbox price is justified here by simpler, auditable single-attempt semantics. Infrastructure failure still stops a phase; it does not authorize another Sandbox or another fit. Any observed unexpected replacement/restart is a failure and closes admission. [Modal preemption](https://modal.com/docs/guide/preemption), [failure semantics](https://modal.com/docs/guide/retries).

Preserve the running transfer controller PID59308, its 35 source bindings, three workers/two threads, and deadline `2026-10-13T15:32:19.924192+00:00`. Do not edit these sources, stop or restart the run, or materialize its mutable output as a supposedly complete cloud input. Old hybrid queues and their success gates remain historical and closed.

The preferred operational implementation is additive: cloud supervisor, certificate validator, bundler, watchdog and cloud-specific worker/report copies. Keep original scripts and protocols byte-identical. Cloud copies may change only the operational seams identified below, with a reviewable source diff and scientific-body equivalence check. Do not globally monkeypatch `os.kill`, `sys.version`, package-version queries, path resolution or the original pipeline's authorization functions.

The twelve pilot fits and 480 scientific fits retain their original logical member identities. Pilot rows never enter scientific artifacts. A filesystem `fsync` or local `completed.json` alone is not a Volume durability acknowledgement. Supervisory hooks may commit immediately around unchanged bundle calls; they must not refit, alter predictions or reorder the graph. Record any fit whose launch is durable but completion is uncertain conservatively. Preserve partial files; no automatic retry or checkpoint continuation is authorized by this design. The existing scientific code's own exclusive attempt files provide an additional guard, not a distributed claim protocol. [Volume commits and visibility](https://modal.com/docs/guide/volumes).

At phase end, the supervisor records true subprocess exit status and artifact hashes, commits them, exits, and the external launcher verifies provider terminal state. One Sandbox per phase means pilot, main and reporting process namespaces differ.
